Autonomous reaction-mechanism exploration requires proposing reaction steps, preparing three-dimensional structures, and determining which reactant and product a computed saddle point connects. Large language model (LLM) agents can misjudge spatial relationships and confuse a computed structure with the species or step they intended to study. We present Cloudra, a multi-agent framework for exploring reaction mechanisms. A Delivery DAG represents the species and elementary steps being studied. Under intelligent restraint, deterministic algorithms match atoms between reactants and products, measure bond lengths and dihedral angles, rotate and translate molecular fragments, and assemble candidate transition-state geometries. Frequency calculations distinguish minima from saddle points, while IRC endpoint calculations identify the species connected by each saddle. These operations let the agents test proposed steps, compare competing pathways, and revise the mechanism. CloudraBench evaluates the framework's end-to-end mechanism-calculation capability across 50 tasks covering elementary reactions, multistep mechanisms, stereochemical competition, and computational method limitations. Across 200 runs under four workflow configurations, Cloudra achieves a mean composite score of 0.793, compared with 0.581 for the same-model Standard baseline. On Hard-tier tasks, the mean score increases from 0.689 to 0.958. The self-consistency component reaches its maximum score in 24 of the 40 non-Impossible tasks, compared with seven under Standard. Behavioral analyses examine how the agents use bond and dihedral measurements, optimized structures, and IRC endpoints to revise local mechanistic assignments.
