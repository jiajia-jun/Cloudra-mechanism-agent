Autonomous reaction-mechanism exploration involves proposing chemical hypotheses, constructing molecular configurations, and determining which species and pathways the calculations establish. Extending large language model (LLM) agents from calculation execution to this scientific process poses challenges in geometric reasoning and mechanistic judgment.

We present Cloudra, a multi-agent framework for reaction-mechanism exploration built around chemical objects. Under intelligent restraint, topology-aware deterministic algorithms map molecular identities and atoms, construct configurations, and translate chemical intent into geometric operations. The Delivery DAG represents the species and elementary steps under investigation, connecting these operations and quantum-chemical calculations to pathway construction, comparison, and revision. Physical checks establish which mechanistic assignments are supported.

CloudraBench evaluates the framework's end-to-end mechanism-calculation capability across 50 tasks covering elementary reactions, multistep mechanisms, stereochemical competition, and computational method limitations. Across 200 runs under four workflow configurations, Cloudra achieves a mean composite score of 0.793, compared with 0.581 for the same-model Standard baseline. On Hard-tier tasks, the mean score increases from 0.689 to 0.958. The self-consistency component reaches its maximum score in 24 of the 40 non-Impossible tasks, compared with seven under Standard. Behavioral analyses examine how explicit geometric measurements and structured result associations affect local scientific judgments.

Cloudra combines geometric reasoning, quantum-chemical computation, and physical validation to construct, compare, and revise candidate mechanisms throughout an autonomous study.
