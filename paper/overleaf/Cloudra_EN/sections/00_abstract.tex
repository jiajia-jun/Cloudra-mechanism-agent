Autonomous exploration of organic reaction mechanisms requires more than successful quantum-chemical calculations: mechanistic conclusions must remain consistent with molecular geometries, reaction pathways, and verifiable physical evidence. Yet large language model (LLM)-based agents face two fundamental challenges: unreliable three-dimensional geometric reasoning and premature claims of scientific completion.

Here, we introduce Cloudra, a physical-contract-driven multi-agent framework that separates mechanistic reasoning, deterministic geometric manipulation, and scientific validation. Following a principle of intelligent restraint, Cloudra delegates spatial operations to topology-aware geometric operators, enabling LLMs to guide molecular transformations through chemical intent rather than direct Cartesian coordinate manipulation. Complementing this geometric framework, a chemical-entity-centered Delivery DAG associates mechanistic claims with archived quantum-chemical artifacts and enforces physical validation of stationary points, reaction pathways, and outstanding scientific requirements.

We evaluate Cloudra on CloudraBench, comprising 50 tasks spanning elementary reactions, multistep mechanisms, stereochemical competition, and computational method limitations. Across 200 runs under four workflow configurations, Cloudra achieves a mean composite score of 0.780, compared with 0.594 for the same-model Standard baseline. On challenging Hard-tier tasks, the mean score increases from 0.689 to 0.958, while full scientific self-consistency scores are achieved in 24 of 40 non-Impossible tasks, compared with seven under Standard. Complementary behavioral analyses reveal how explicit geometric measurements and structured evidence relationships support more reliable local scientific judgments.

These results establish a framework for autonomous mechanism exploration in which computational execution is coupled to explicit geometric operations and verifiable scientific evidence, shifting the focus from completing calculations to substantiating mechanistic conclusions.
